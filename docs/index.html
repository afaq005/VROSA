VROSA

Abstract

Mobile robots deployed in manufacturing, logistics, inspection, and service settings are largely confined to pre-programmed tasks; adapting behaviour to a new instruction usually means engineering time, reprogramming, and redeployment. VROSA is a vision-language agent framework, built on ROS 2, that lets an operator issue natural language commands directly: an LLM planner selects from a constrained tool registry rather than generating raw motor commands, keeping semantic reasoning and physical execution separate for safety and auditability. The system pairs a hot-swappable vision-language model (VLM) backend (cloud, local, or remote) with a camera-first perception pipeline (YOLO + GroundingDINO + Depth Anything V2) that estimates object distance without requiring LiDAR. A closed-loop plan–execute–recheck navigation cycle alternates sparse VLM direction queries with dense, sensor-driven motion steps. Across 50 simulation trials and 24 outdoor hardware trials on a physical AgileX Scout 2.0, VROSA reaches a 93.75% partial-credit navigation success rate with zero agent, tool, or perception code changed when porting from simulation to hardware.

NAVplan_execute_recheck

One VLM call per leg. Every step in between is sensor-only.

navigate_to_object runs a plan–execute–recheck loop: a single VLM call ("scout") returns a coarse direction and distance; the robot then drives a portion of that estimated distance in small sensor-checked sub-steps depth and, when available, LiDAR: with no further VLM calls until the leg completes or an obstacle intervenes. Real hardware trials average roughly one VLM query per metre travelled, not per motion step.

Off the sim floor, onto a Korean university campus.

Physical trials ran on an AgileX Scout 2.0 four-wheel skid-steer UGV with a Jetson AGX Orin 64GB running all perception and agent computation onboard; camera-only, no LiDAR. Twenty-four navigation trials spanned three outdoor sessions (midday sun and glare, evening, early morning), targets from 0.5–23.2 m, cluttered e-bike/scooter zones, a Korean-text no-parking sign commanded in English, and a person who moved mid-navigation.

Sim vs. hardware

The hardware partial-credit NSR of 93.75% is consistent with the simulation depth-only baseline (87%), suggesting outdoor natural lighting does not degrade monocular depth estimation relative to indoor simulation conditions. The higher VLM query count (6.4 vs 2.0) and longer completion time reflect the substantially greater navigation distances in hardware trials (mean 7.7 m start distance vs 2–4 m in simulation). A consistent odometry overshoot on the Scout 2.0 skid-steer chassis was observed and is correctable with a single calibration coefficient.

VLMbackend_manager

Format compliance matters more than description quality.

Three VLM backends were compared under identical fused-mode conditions (50 sim trials each). Claude Sonnet, LLaVA V5, and Phi-4 Base landed within a few points of each other on navigation success; the differences aren't statistically significant at this sample size. What did separate them was direction-query format compliance, which tracked completion time and attempt count far more tightly than raw success rate.

LLaVA V5's near-parity with the cloud backend, despite running fully offline, is the operationally relevant result; it makes runtime backend switching a genuine connectivity/cost trade-off rather than a pure performance one.

Human-rated scene-description quality tells a complementary story: Phi-4 and Claude Sonnet produced richer descriptions (mean 4.5 and 4.6 out of 5), while LLaVA V5 led on format compliance (4.8). This supports runtime VLM switching; a local fine-tuned model for structured navigation, a cloud model for rich scene interpretation.

Where it breaks

SENSOR MODALITY DEPENDENCY

With depth, YOLO, and LiDAR all disabled, VLM-only distance estimation is unreliable enough to prevent arrival confirmation. At least one sensing channel is required.

SMALL OBJECTS + DIRECT GLARE

The single confirmed hardware failure: a cola can on a white line under direct midday sun. Same target succeeded in shade. High-contrast glare, not low light, is the issue.

REFLECTIVE SURFACES

Metallic objects produce larger depth errors and can trigger early (safe, but imprecise) safety stops from shortened LiDAR returns in simulation.

VLM DIRECTION INSTABILITY

Visually symmetric scenes (matching shelves either side of a corridor) can cause the VLM to alternate direction labels across queries.
